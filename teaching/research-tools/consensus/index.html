Guide 04 / Compare, then check

Consensus

Search with a research question, inspect the sources and build your own comparison. Use AI synthesis as a reading aid, not a verdict.

6 stepsFree Paper searchPro and Deep are optional and limited

  1. Start with the free Paper search

    Input
    Our question about immigration and local wages.
    Action
    Open Consensus and use a free account if prompted. Choose Paper search under Tools in the bottom-left menu, rather than the default Pro mode. Enter the question below.
    Output
    A list of candidate papers; no AI-generated table is required.
    First search query

    How does an immigration inflow affect local workers' wages?

    Refine the search without spending analysis credits

    Run another Paper search for Mariel boatlift wages, then try immigration firms labour supply adjustment. These are search formulations, not requests for an AI report.

    The current documentation places Paper search under Tools and says the main search defaults to Pro. Labels can change: check the selected mode. Official search instructions.

  2. Select three sources and check their identity

    Input
    The candidate list.
    Action
    Open three promising titles, then their source links. Confirm authors, date, version and abstract. Search a missing known paper by title or DOI; cross-check coverage through Scholar or IDEAS.
    Output
    Three real sources with their reading and access limits identified.

    Consensus is not a guarantee of working-paper coverage. If you try the optional Snapshot tab in a paper's sidebar, verify its extracted details. Missing fields mean you need to check the source, not that the paper omits the information. Snapshot instructions.

  3. Build a three-paper comparison

    Input
    Your sources and the blank comparison matrix.
    Action
    Enter the question, population or model, method, finding and connection to your project. Attach a source link and location. Fill only what you checked; write not yet checked for unread assumptions.
    Output
    A source-linked matrix, without paid extraction or export.
    Optional: use one Deep review for an initial table

    If you have an unused free allowance, choose Deep in the main search bar and adapt this request. Check which papers and versions it actually uses; a requested paper is not a guarantee of retrieval.

    Optional Deep request

    Compare the Mariel boatlift studies by Card (1990), Borjas (2017), and Peri and Yasenov (2019). Give source links and a table of populations, methods, author-reported findings and important differences. Distinguish abstract-based statements from full-text checks. Do not infer missing assumptions or treat a statistically insignificant estimate as proof of zero effect.

    Deep can produce tables. An inline citation checkmark indicates full-text use, not independent verification. Deep also uses a Pro message credit. Deep instructions and source indicators.

    A curated comparison to inspect

    Teaching example, not a Consensus output or a full literature review

    These entries stop at what the linked abstracts support. Connections are our interpretations; detailed design appraisal remains to be checked.

    Card (1990)

    Question/data
    Mariel's labour-market effects; Current Population Survey, Miami.
    Author reports
    Little response in less-skilled workers' wages or unemployment.
    Connection
    Direct starting point for this question.
    Evidence
    Published abstract, printed p. 245 (author's copy). Design assumptions: not yet checked.

    Borjas (2017)

    Question/sample
    Re-examines Mariel, focusing on high-school dropouts.
    Author reports
    A substantial wage decline for that low-skill group.
    Connection
    A contrasting result; inspect samples and design before explaining disagreement.
    Evidence
    Published abstract on the author's institutional page. Detailed assumptions: not yet checked.

    Peri and Yasenov (2019)

    Method/sample
    Synthetic control, non-Cuban high-school dropouts.
    Author reports
    No statistically significant wage difference relative to the control after 1980.
    Connection
    A methodological re-examination.
    Evidence
    Published abstract, printed p. 267. Exact identifying assumptions: not yet checked.

  4. Check one cell and repair an overclaim

    Input
    The illustrative claim: “Immigration has no effect on wages.”
    Action
    Read the Peri and Yasenov abstract. Restore the population, comparison and uncertainty the claim removes.
    Output
    A narrower, evidence-linked sentence.
    Which sentence matches the abstract?

    Answer and next check

    The second sentence preserves the study's scope and statistical uncertainty. It reports the authors' finding; it does not endorse their identification. Read the methods and results next.

    The overclaim is invented, not an actual Consensus response. A Consensus Meter or a majority of retrieved findings is not a causal verdict for your setting.

  5. Keep the matrix and choose two readings

    Input
    Your corrected comparison.
    Action
    Save your checked notes in the matrix. Choose one closest paper and one that challenges your current view. Write a specific reading question for each.
    Output
    A starting matrix and two purposeful reading tasks.

    For example: “How is the comparison constructed?” Save references to Zotero from their source pages. If available, Consensus's RIS export is another route; exporting references does not create your checked matrix. Zotero and export workflows.

  6. Repeat with your own question

    Input
    Your current research question.
    Action
    Use it in Paper search, then repeat the source and matrix checks. The longer request below is optional for Pro or Deep. For theory, compare model primitives, assumptions and propositions instead of empirical data and effects.
    Output
    A comparison to discuss with your supervisor, not a final contribution claim.

    This page does not send or save your question. Do not paste confidential drafts or restricted data into external tools.

    Optional Pro / Deep request

    My research question is: [insert your question]. Find five candidate economics papers connected by question, mechanism or method. Include working papers where relevant and a potentially conflicting result. For each, give a source link, the connection, and whether you used an abstract or full text. Do not claim the list is exhaustive or that my question is novel.

Stop here

Read the two selected papers before adding more rows. A promising connection is a reading lead; a contribution needs a checked comparison.
