Guide 04 / Compare, then check

Elicit

Describe the question in ordinary language. Use the comparison to decide what to read, not what to believe.

6 stepsAccount requiredUsage and export limits depend on plan

  1. Ask a small, clear research question

    Input
    Our question about immigration and local wages.
    Action
    Open Elicit, sign in and start a Research Agent session. Ask for a few papers, not a complete literature review.
    Output
    A first list of candidate sources with links.
    First request

    How does an immigration inflow affect local workers' wages? Find five candidate economics papers, including studies of the Mariel boatlift. Give a source link and explain each paper's connection. Include working papers where relevant.

    Refine the search, rather than accepting the first list

    Try a follow-up: “Also look for a conflicting finding and work on how firms adjust to a labour supply shock. Distinguish connections by question, mechanism and method.”

    The current workflow combines discovery and extraction in the Agent; older tutorials may show separate tools. Current getting-started guide.

  2. Select three sources and check their identity

    Input
    The candidate list.
    Action
    Open three promising source links. Check author, title, date and version. If a known close paper is missing, find it independently and provide its verified reference. Do not assume an Agent has read the full text.
    Output
    Three real, relevant sources with their reading/access limits identified.

    Where you have permission, an accessible PDF can improve extraction. If only an abstract is available, label it. Do not upload confidential drafts or restricted material. Full-text and extraction help.

  3. Request a small comparison

    Input
    Your three selected papers.
    Action
    Ask the Agent for a table, then add or revise a column if the answers are too vague. Column instructions.
    Output
    A provisional matrix with source locations and explicit gaps.
    Comparison request

    Compare only these three selected papers. Include: paper/version and link; question; data, population or model; method and key assumptions; author-reported finding; connection to my question; source page, section or table; abstract-only or full-text basis. Separate your interpretation from the authors' claims. If the available text does not establish a detail, write "not yet checked" rather than guessing.

    A curated comparison to inspect

    Teaching example, not an Elicit output or a full literature review

    These entries deliberately stop at what the linked abstracts support. The proposed connections are our interpretations. Assumptions and detailed design appraisal remain to be checked.

    Card (1990)

    Question/data
    Mariel's labour-market effects; Current Population Survey, Miami.
    Author reports
    Little response in less-skilled workers' wages or unemployment.
    Connection
    Direct starting point for this question.
    Evidence
    Published abstract, printed p. 245 (author's copy). Design assumptions: not yet checked.

    Borjas (2017)

    Question/sample
    Re-examines Mariel, focusing on high-school dropouts.
    Author reports
    A substantial wage decline for that low-skill group.
    Connection
    A contrasting result; compare the samples and design before explaining the disagreement.
    Evidence
    Published abstract, reproduced on the author's institutional page. Detailed assumptions: not yet checked.

    Peri and Yasenov (2019)

    Source examined
    Published abstract, printed p. 267; not the full text.
    Method/sample
    Synthetic control, non-Cuban high-school dropouts.
    Author reports
    No statistically significant wage difference relative to the control after 1980.
    Connection
    A methodological re-examination. Exact identifying assumptions: not yet checked.

  4. Check one cell and repair an overclaim

    Input
    The illustrative claim: “Immigration has no effect on wages.”
    Action
    Read the linked Peri and Yasenov published abstract. Restore the population, comparison and uncertainty that the claim removes.
    Output
    A narrower, evidence-linked sentence.
    Which sentence matches the abstract?

    Answer and next check

    The second sentence preserves the study's scope and the distinction between “not statistically significant” and “exactly zero”. It is a report of the authors' finding, not our endorsement of the identification.

    Next, read the relevant methods and results sections. An abstract can support this narrow summary, not a detailed assessment of assumptions.

    The overclaim is invented for this exercise; it is not attributed to an actual Elicit response.

  5. Keep the matrix and choose two readings

    Input
    Your corrected comparison.
    Action
    Keep the three rows in the blank comparison template. Choose two papers: one closest to your question and one that most challenges your current view. Write a specific reading question for each.
    Output
    A checked starting matrix and two purposeful reading tasks.

    Example tasks: “How is the comparison constructed?” and “Which sample definition changes the interpretation?” If your plan permits table export, use it; otherwise enter your checked notes manually. Export access and formats.

  6. Repeat with your own question

    Input
    Your current research question.
    Action
    Adapt the request below, find a few candidates, then repeat the source and cell checks. For theory work, substitute model primitives, assumptions and propositions for empirical data and effects.
    Output
    A first comparison to discuss with your supervisor, not a final contribution claim.

    This page does not send or save your question. Copying it into an external tool is a separate choice.

    Adaptable discovery request

    My research question is: [insert your question]. Find five candidate economics papers connected by question, mechanism or method. Include working papers where relevant and a potentially conflicting result. For each, give a source link, the connection, and whether you used an abstract or full text. Do not claim the list is exhaustive or that my question is novel.

Stop here

Read the two selected papers before adding more rows. A promising connection is a reading lead; a contribution needs a checked comparison.
